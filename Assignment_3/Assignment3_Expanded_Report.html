Assignment 3: Weather Forecasting — Expanded Experiments

Prepared by Aamir Sohaib

Jena Climate 2009–2016: predict temperature 24 hours ahead from 120 hourly observations (five days).

Method

Original 10-minute data aggregated to hourly means; invalid/missing readings handled using past values only. Chronological 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits and training-only scaling. Nine neural models (including four stacked recurrent configurations) compared with a seasonal-persistence baseline. Neural networks used Adam, MAE loss, up to 15 epochs, batch size 256 and early stopping.

Results interpretation

64 units raised GRU MAE by 0.089°C versus 32 units. 64 units lowered LSTM MAE by 0.059°C versus 32 units.

Best stacked setup: Stacked GRU 64-32 (2.401°C validation MAE). Best non-stacked recurrent setup: GRU 32 (2.380°C). These results show how changes to recurrent depth and unit order affected this dataset.

Conclusions and limitations

Both individual recurrent layers and stacked configurations were evaluated, including changing unit counts and their order within two-layer GRU and LSTM models. Model selection used validation error only, and test MAE was reported for every fixed configuration afterward.

This is a single seeded, chronological-split comparison. Neural training has variability, and short training budgets can affect the ranking. Full test results are descriptive: they were not used to revise the validation-selected winner. Results are specific to this weather dataset and setup.
